## Executive Summary

8.8 Events should launch as a hybrid online marketplace for event vendors in Nigeria. Customers should be able to browse, request quotes, and book vendors on their own, while the 8.8 Events admin team can monitor activity, verify vendors, support quote matching, intervene in disputes, and help customers who want assisted planning support.

The strongest wedge is trust. Nigerian event hosts currently rely heavily on WhatsApp, Instagram, referrals, and fragmented vendor contacts. That makes discovery, comparison, coordination, payment confidence, and accountability difficult, especially for first-time hosts, out-of-town planners, and corporate event buyers.

The first version should not try to become a fully automated national marketplace immediately. It should run a four-state pilot with a sequenced rollout: prepare vendor supply in all four states, then promote demand state by state as each state reaches minimum vendor readiness. The operating model should stay tight: verified vendors, structured quotes, admin-visible bookings, protected booking payments through licensed payment partners, post-event feedback, and strong support workflows.

## Problem Statement

People planning events in Nigeria often struggle to find reliable vendors, compare prices, confirm quality, coordinate delivery, and pay with confidence. Vendors also struggle to build trust beyond social media pages, word of mouth, and informal referrals.

The result is a high-stress planning experience where customers fear fraud, no-shows, inflated pricing, poor communication, and weak accountability. For high-stakes events such as weddings, corporate events, birthdays, launches, and social celebrations, one vendor failure can damage the entire event.

## Target Users

The initial customer focus should be:

- First-time event hosts who do not already have a trusted vendor network.
- Out-of-town customers planning events in one of the launch states.
- Busy professionals who want vendor discovery and booking support without managing dozens of informal chats.
- Corporate or brand event buyers who need reliability, documentation, and vendor accountability.

The initial vendor focus should be:

- MCs.
- DJs.
- Catering and food vendors.
- Event planning services.
- Makeup and styling vendors.
- Printing and branding vendors.
- Fashion designers who make wedding gowns, suits, and event outfits.

The launch geography should follow a geopolitical-zone expansion logic so 8.8 Events does not become locked into one region. The first confirmed launch locations are Abuja/FCT, Kano, and Lagos. The fourth launch state should be selected from another geopolitical zone based on vendor supply, event demand, and the team's ability to verify vendors locally.

The remaining fourth launch state should be finalized by the founder based on vendor supply, customer demand, operational reach, local event volume, and geopolitical-zone coverage. The plan assumes four initial launch locations rather than a Lagos-only launch.

## Core Decisions

- Launch as a hybrid marketplace, not concierge-only and not fully self-service-only.
- Start across four Nigerian launch locations selected from different geopolitical zones where possible, with Abuja/FCT, Kano, and Lagos already confirmed and one additional state still to be selected.
- Use licensed payment partners and clear booking terms instead of 8.8 Events directly holding customer funds in the first MVP.
- Make trust the first product promise: verified vendors, transparent quotes, structured booking records, support, reviews, and dispute handling.
- Build admin visibility from day one so the team can see user activity, vendor responses, quotes, bookings, payment status, disputes, and customer support needs.

## Premises

- Customers will use an event marketplace if it reduces vendor discovery stress and increases payment confidence.
- Vendors will join if the platform gives them qualified leads, better credibility, and a clearer booking pipeline.
- A four-state launch can work if the platform controls vendor onboarding and does not spread support too thin.
- A hybrid model is more realistic than pure automation at the start because event planning has emotional, cultural, and operational complexity.
- Payment trust should be built through licensed payment infrastructure, milestone or booking rules, receipts, refund rules, and dispute workflows.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Assisted-First Marketplace

The platform exists, but most successful bookings are manually supported by the 8.8 Events team at the start.

Why it was not chosen: it is good for learning, but it does not fully match the founder's intention to create an online platform where users can also self-serve.

### Approach B: Hybrid Marketplace With Admin Support

Users can browse, request quotes, compare vendors, message, and book self-service. Admins can monitor the marketplace, verify vendors, support customers, help with quote matching, manage disputes, and step in when a booking needs attention.

Why it was chosen: it gives 8.8 Events the feel of a real online marketplace from day one while preserving the human support needed to build trust in an early vendor network.

### Approach C: Full Self-Service Marketplace

Users and vendors handle most discovery, booking, payment, and coordination themselves.

Why it was not chosen: it is scalable, but risky before vendor quality, response times, reviews, dispute rules, and state-level operations are proven.

## Recommended Product Model

8.8 Events should be designed around three user groups:

1. Customers who need vendors for an event.
2. Vendors who want verified leads and bookings.
3. Admins who manage trust, support, verification, disputes, and marketplace quality.

The customer experience should allow a user to:

- Create an account.
- Select event type, event date, state, city, budget range, and needed services.
- Search or browse verified vendors.
- Request quotes from selected vendors.
- Receive vendor responses and compare offers.
- Ask for 8.8 Events support if they want help.
- Confirm a booking through a protected payment flow.
- Track booking status.
- Leave reviews after the event.

The vendor experience should allow a vendor to:

- Apply for verification.
- Create a profile with services, packages, location coverage, portfolio, price ranges, availability, and policies.
- Receive quote requests.
- Respond with structured quotes.
- Confirm or decline booking requests.
- Track payment status and booking obligations.
- Receive reviews and build marketplace reputation.

The admin experience should allow 8.8 Events to:

- Approve, reject, suspend, or re-verify vendors.
- Review customer requests and vendor responses.
- See marketplace activity by state, category, booking stage, and payment status.
- Step into chats or support tickets.
- Manage disputes and refund workflows.
- Track vendor response times, completion rates, ratings, and complaints.
- Identify supply gaps by state and service category.

## Payment And Trust Model

The first MVP should avoid language or operations that imply 8.8 Events is personally holding customer money as an unlicensed financial institution. The safer framing is protected booking payments through licensed payment partners, with clear booking terms and milestone or release rules.

Recommended payment rules:

- Customer pays through an approved payment provider.
- Platform records the booking agreement, amount, vendor, event date, and cancellation terms.
- Vendor payout rules are visible before confirmation.
- Customer and vendor both receive receipts and booking records.
- Disputes are handled through documented evidence: quote, chat, contract terms, delivery proof, and customer complaint.
- Refund and cancellation rules are shown before payment.

This gives customers a trust signal without making the first version dependent on 8.8 Events operating as a regulated funds custodian.

Relevant regulatory context: the Central Bank of Nigeria supervises payment systems, payment service providers, mobile money, bill payments, electronic payment channels, and related risk controls. This supports the recommendation to partner with licensed payment infrastructure rather than build direct custody first.

### Payment And Refund Policy For Nigeria

The first payment policy should be built around licensed Nigerian payment infrastructure such as Paystack, Flutterwave, or another provider listed in the CBN payment service provider ecosystem. 8.8 Events should not directly hold customer funds as an informal escrow operator in the MVP.

Recommended customer-facing payment policy:

- Customers pay through the platform's approved payment provider or payment link.
- Every payment must be tied to a booking record, vendor, customer, amount, event date, and service description.
- The customer must see cancellation, refund, and dispute terms before paying.
- Platform fees and payment processing fees should be disclosed before checkout.
- 8.8 Events should maintain an internal payment reference for reconciliation, support, disputes, and reporting.
- Vendor payout should follow the agreed booking terms, service delivery status, and payment-provider settlement rules.

Recommended refund policy:

- Full refund if the vendor cancels before service delivery and no replacement vendor is accepted by the customer.
- Full or partial refund if the vendor materially fails to deliver the agreed service, based on evidence and admin review.
- Partial refund if the customer cancels after the vendor has already incurred documented preparation costs.
- No automatic refund for customer no-show or late cancellation after the stated cutoff, unless the vendor agrees or the policy allows it.
- Processing fees may be non-refundable where the payment provider does not return those fees.
- Refunds should be processed through the same payment provider whenever possible so transaction records stay clean.

Recommended dispute policy:

- Customers and vendors can open a dispute from the booking page.
- Evidence should include quote, booking terms, receipts, chat records, event date, delivery proof, photos, and cancellation messages.
- Admin should review disputes using a documented checklist.
- Chargeback notices from the payment provider must be treated as urgent because Nigerian payment providers may require fast merchant responses.
- Admin should respond to provider disputes with evidence through the provider dashboard or API where available.
- 8.8 Events should track dispute reasons, resolution time, refund amount, and vendor history.

Recommended payout policy:

- New vendors should receive payouts only after booking completion is confirmed or after the dispute window defined in the platform policy.
- Trusted vendors may later qualify for faster payout based on completed bookings, low dispute rate, and strong ratings.
- Vendors with open disputes, repeated cancellations, or suspicious activity should have payouts paused pending review.
- Commission should be calculated and recorded before payout.

This policy should be reviewed by a Nigerian lawyer or compliance advisor before public launch, especially if 8.8 Events wants to advertise "escrow" or hold funds directly in the future.

## MVP Scope

The pilot MVP should include:

- Customer signup and login.
- Vendor signup and verification application.
- Vendor profiles and service listings.
- State and category search.
- Event request form.
- Quote request and quote response workflow.
- Admin dashboard for vendor approval and marketplace monitoring.
- Booking confirmation flow.
- Payment-provider integration or payment-link workflow.
- Booking status tracking.
- Support or dispute ticketing, even if handled partly through admin notes at first.

The pilot can handle these manually before they become product features:

- Public reviews and advanced ratings.
- Vendor subscriptions and featured listings.
- Automated vendor ranking.
- Advanced quote comparison.
- AI matching.
- Corporate account management.

The MVP should not initially include:

- Full event-planner project management tools.
- Complex social media features.
- Nationwide vendor onboarding without state-level quality control.
- Direct in-house escrow custody.
- Advanced AI matching before enough real booking data exists.

## Pilot Definition

The first build should prove the marketplace workflow before it tries to automate every part of event planning.

Non-negotiable pilot decisions:

- Select the exact four launch states before vendor onboarding begins.
- Pick the first 5 to 7 vendor categories.
- Define the minimum vendor verification standard.
- Choose the payment partner or payment-link workflow.
- Publish simple cancellation, refund, and dispute rules before the first paid booking.
- Decide whether assisted booking is free during the pilot or charged as a premium service.

Must be built before pilot:

- Customer event request form.
- Vendor profile pages.
- Admin vendor approval.
- Admin view of customer requests, vendor quotes, booking status, and support notes.
- Booking record with customer, vendor, event date, amount, terms, and payment status.
- Payment link or payment-provider handoff.

Can be manual during pilot:

- Vendor matching.
- Quote follow-up.
- Customer support.
- Dispute intake.
- Post-event feedback collection.
- Vendor ranking decisions.

Key edge cases to define before launch:

- Vendor no-show.
- Vendor partial delivery.
- Customer changes event date.
- Vendor becomes unavailable after accepting.
- Customer cancels after vendor has started preparation.
- Vendor attempts to take payment outside the platform.
- Fake or manipulated reviews.
- Vendor impersonation.
- Failed or partial payment.
- Duplicate bookings or vendor overbooking.

## Build Structure

This section translates the business blueprint into a practical structure for developers, designers, and no-code builders. It is based on the uploaded platform blueprint, adjusted for the approved hybrid marketplace direction and the payment decision from this office-hours session.

### Product Surfaces

8.8 Events should be built as four connected surfaces:

1. Public website for discovery, search, vendor profiles, education, and vendor registration.
2. Customer dashboard for event requests, saved vendors, bookings, messages, payments, reviews, and support.
3. Vendor dashboard for profile setup, service catalog, quote requests, bookings, calendar, messages, earnings, reviews, and subscriptions.
4. Admin dashboard for user management, vendor verification, booking oversight, dispute support, financial reporting, content management, marketing tools, and platform settings.

The admin dashboard is not optional. Because the launch model is hybrid, admins must be able to observe and support the marketplace from day one.

### Recommended Technical Architecture

The blueprint recommends a layered architecture:

- Client layer: React web app, responsive mobile web, future Flutter mobile app, and React admin dashboard.
- API layer: RESTful API using Node.js, Express, and TypeScript.
- Application services: user service, vendor service, booking service, payment service, search service, review service, notification service, and admin service.
- Data layer: PostgreSQL as the primary database, Redis for sessions/cache/rate limits, and Cloudinary or AWS S3 for media storage.
- Third-party services: Paystack as primary payment provider, Flutterwave as backup, SendGrid or AWS SES for email, Termii or Twilio for SMS, Firebase for push notifications, Google Maps for location, and a KYC or document-verification partner where needed.

Recommended stack:

| Layer | Recommended tools |
| --- | --- |
| Web frontend | React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS |
| Admin dashboard | React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS |
| Mobile app | Flutter, after web MVP is proven |
| Backend API | Node.js, Express, TypeScript |
| Database | PostgreSQL |
| Cache | Redis |
| Search | Algolia or Elasticsearch |
| Media storage | Cloudinary or AWS S3 |
| Payments | Paystack primary, Flutterwave backup |
| Authentication | JWT plus optional Google or Apple OAuth |
| Email | SendGrid or AWS SES |
| SMS | Termii or Twilio |
| Push notifications | Firebase Cloud Messaging |
| Hosting | Vercel for frontend, AWS or DigitalOcean for backend |
| CI/CD | GitHub Actions |
| Monitoring | Sentry plus Datadog, New Relic, or a lighter equivalent |

For a no-code or low-code first version, the same structure can be mapped to Bubble, FlutterFlow, Xano, Supabase, Airtable, Make, and Paystack payment links. The key is preserving the same user roles, booking records, vendor verification flow, and admin visibility.

### Vendor Onboarding Flow

1. Registration.
   Vendor enters business name, email, phone, password, primary service category, and accepts terms.

2. Email or phone verification.
   Account is activated after verification.

3. Business profile setup.
   Vendor uploads business logo, writes business description, adds address and service areas, uploads CAC or business document where available, uploads valid ID, adds payout bank account, and sets hours or availability.

4. Service catalog creation.
   Vendor adds packages, descriptions, prices, price type, service areas, travel fees, portfolio images, and optional videos.

5. Verification review.
   Admin reviews documents, calls vendor where needed, checks portfolio credibility, and approves, rejects, or requests changes.

6. Go live.
   Approved vendor profile becomes visible, vendor receives onboarding email, and vendor can receive quote or booking requests.

## Four-State Launch Strategy

The launch geography should be selected from geopolitical zones so the platform can prove that the model works beyond one regional market. The first confirmed locations are:

- Abuja/FCT.
- Kano.
- Lagos.
- One additional state to be selected from another geopolitical zone.

The fourth state should be chosen using these criteria:

- Strong event volume.
- Enough reliable vendors to onboard before launch.
- Founder or team ability to verify vendors locally.
- Customer willingness to book vendors online.
- Logistics feasibility for event categories that require physical delivery.
- Representation from a geopolitical zone not already covered by Abuja/FCT, Kano, and Lagos where possible.

For each launch state, 8.8 Events should aim to onboard a minimum viable vendor base before pushing customer acquisition:

- 20 to 30 verified vendors per state.
- Coverage across at least 5 to 7 major service categories.
- At least 3 to 5 strong vendors per key category.
- Response-time expectations agreed with every vendor.
- Clear quality standards and removal rules.

## Go-To-Market Plan

The launch should start with trust-heavy acquisition channels:

- Instagram and TikTok vendor spotlights.
- WhatsApp referral loops for customers and vendors.
- Partnerships with photographers, makeup artists, decorators, venues, and planners.
- Campus, church, community, and corporate event networks.
- SEO pages for event vendor searches by state and category.
- Paid social campaigns around high-intent moments such as weddings, birthdays, corporate events, and launches.

The strongest early message:

"Find verified event vendors, compare quotes, and book with support when you need it."

This is clearer and safer than leading with "escrow" as the main promise.

## Business Model

The business can combine:

- Commission on successful bookings.
- Vendor subscription plans for higher visibility or lead access.
- Featured listings for verified vendors.
- Assisted booking service fees for customers who want extra support.
- Corporate or premium event support packages.

The early business model should favor transaction learning over maximizing fees. The first priority is proving that customers book, vendors respond, events complete successfully, and disputes are manageable.

## Success Criteria

The first 90 days should measure:

- Number of verified vendors onboarded per state.
- Customer event requests submitted.
- Quote request to vendor response rate.
- Average vendor response time.
- Quote request to booking conversion rate.
- Booking completion rate.
- Dispute rate.
- Refund or cancellation rate.
- Customer satisfaction after event completion.
- Repeat customer or referral rate.
- Vendor retention and vendor-paid conversion.

A strong first milestone would be:

- Four states launched with controlled vendor supply.
- At least 80 to 120 verified vendors total.
- At least 100 customer event requests.
- At least 25 completed bookings.
- Vendor response rate above 70%.
- Completed booking satisfaction above 80%.

## Key Risks

- Launching in four states may stretch vendor verification and support capacity.
- Vendors may join but fail to respond quickly or professionally.
- Customers may browse but still prefer informal WhatsApp referrals.
- Payment trust may be difficult to communicate without overpromising escrow.
- Disputes may require more operational time than expected.
- Vendor quality may vary widely by state and category.
- A broad category launch may weaken the initial marketplace experience if some categories have thin supply.

## Risk Controls

- Launch state by state inside the four-state plan, even if the brand message says four states.
- Verify vendors before public visibility.
- Start with clear service categories and quality standards.
- Use response-time SLAs for vendors.
- Give admins strong monitoring tools.
- Use payment partners and documented booking terms.
- Keep dispute policies simple and visible.
- Remove or downrank vendors who repeatedly fail customers.

## Roadmap

### Phase 1: Preparation

- Select the four launch states.
- Define vendor categories for launch.
- Create vendor verification checklist.
- Recruit initial vendors.
- Build customer event request flow.
- Build admin dashboard foundations.
- Choose payment partner and booking terms.

### Phase 2: Controlled Launch

- Open customer requests in the four states.
- Allow self-service browsing and quote requests.
- Use admin support heavily behind the scenes.
- Track every drop-off point in the booking journey.
- Manually help customers where the product is not yet strong.

### Phase 3: Marketplace Strengthening

- Improve vendor ranking based on response and completion data.
- Add better state and category pages.
- Improve reviews, portfolios, and quote comparison.
- Add customer reminders and vendor follow-up automation.
- Introduce paid vendor plans only after vendors see real lead value.

### Phase 4: Expansion

- Add more states once the first four have stable vendor supply and customer demand.
- Expand event categories carefully.
- Add planner tools or corporate packages after the core booking flow works.
- Consider deeper payment or escrow-like features only after legal and payment-provider review.

## The Assignment

Before building the full product, recruit and verify 20 vendors in each of the four proposed launch states. Then run 20 real customer event requests through a manual or semi-manual version of the marketplace.

The goal is not just to see whether people like the idea. The goal is to see whether vendors respond, customers trust the process, bookings close, and support problems are manageable.
